# DECISIONS.md

1. **Indexes created and why**
   - `employees`: Unique index on `emp_code` (prevents duplicates, fast lookups). Index on `department` (for filtering and department-based analytics).
   - `attendance_logs`: Unique index on `(emp_code, date)` (prevents race conditions on punch-in, fast single-record lookups). Index on `(date, emp_code)` (optimizes the `/attendance` list endpoint's default sort). Index on `(emp_code, punch_in)` (optimizes employee monthly analytics).

2. **Identical punch-ins at the exact same instant**
   - The unique compound index on `(emp_code, date)` guarantees atomic inserts. The first operation succeeds (201), while the second fails with a `DuplicateKeyError` which the API catches and translates into a 409 response.

3. **Leaderboard ties at the cutoff**
   - The `$setWindowFields` aggregation stage computes standard competition `$rank`. A `$match` stage then filters where `rank <= limit`. If the limit is 10 and three people tie for 10th place, all three are returned, making the result length 12.

4. **Department summary counting employees with no logs**
   - The aggregation pipeline begins on the `employees` collection, not the attendance logs. After filtering out future joiners, it uses a `$lookup` to join attendance records. Employees without logs end up with an empty matched array, but remain in the pipeline to be counted in the headcount.

5. **Scaling for 100x the data**
   - At ~10M records, `$skip` and `$limit` pagination degrades heavily; I would switch to keyset/cursor-based pagination.
   - Dynamic aggregations across 10M records for monthly/trend reports would be slow. I would introduce pre-calculated rollups (materialized views) generated by a nightly cron job or MongoDB Triggers.
